# Working in this repository

**What this is:** a small, reproducible research repository and write-up on persona stability in
language models, supporting an application with a fixed deadline of **22 November 2026**. The
goal is *one rigorous, honest, reproducible result* — not a broad survey. Scope creep is the
main failure mode.

**This repository is public.** It was kept private until the study was complete and the
pre-publication checks had passed. Write every file as though anyone will read it, because
anyone can.

---

## 1. Start from the pre-registration

Read `PREREGISTRATION.md` before doing anything. It holds the question, the statistic, the gates,
the rubric and the decisions, and its section 15 lists every departure from it. Reimplement
methods from the published papers it cites rather than copying code, and if you think you need
something that is not in this repository, stop and ask.

---

## 2. Stop at the checkpoints

The study has five **⏸ REVIEW** checkpoints with the owner. They are not advisory. Stop, present what you have,
and wait for the owner.

The first one gates everything: the research question, pre-registration, labelling rubric and
budget must be approved before any instance is started, any paid API is called or any data is
collected.

**`PREREGISTRATION.md` must be marked FINAL and committed before any labelling begins.** Its git
history is the evidence of when each commitment was made — that is the whole point of keeping it
in version control. Labelling first and writing the rubric afterwards destroys the study's
credibility, and no result is worth that.

---

## 3. Hard exclusions

**Allow-list, not a block-list.** This repository may contain only:

- traits, roles and methods drawn from the published papers cited in the pre-registration
- public datasets, where their licence permits it
- conversations newly generated for this study, with prompts and seeds recorded

Anything else needs the owner's explicit approval before it lands, and that includes derived
artifacts — prompts, vectors, adapters, labels and numeric results — not just raw data.

The specific excluded terms are kept in a local term list that is never committed (see the
`safety-check` target in the `Makefile`). They are not restated here on purpose: enumerating what a public repository avoids
describes the thing it is avoiding, which is itself the disclosure this rule exists to prevent.
An allow-list says everything a block-list would, and reveals nothing.

**Run the scan before any publish-facing milestone:**

```bash
make safety-check SAFETY_TERMS=<path to the local term list>
```

Review every hit by hand. A term may legitimately appear in its ordinary English sense in a
paper-derived context; that is a judgement call, not an automatic pass.

Framing rule: motivate everything in terms of **deployment reliability and safety** — an
assistant that holds its persona and its honesty over a long conversation, and a monitor worth
trusting. Never in terms of engagement, retention, session length or personality-matching.

---

## 4. Reproducibility is a hard requirement

- One command per stage, through the `Makefile`. If a stage cannot be run from a clean clone, it
  is not done.
- Seeds fixed and logged. Environment pinned before anything lands in `results/`.
- **Every figure in the write-up is generated by a script in `src/analysis/` from files in
  `results/`.** No hand-made figures, no numbers typed into prose.
- Large artifacts — activations, checkpoints, intermediate generations — are never committed.
  Document how to regenerate them in `DATA_AND_MODELS.md`.
- Record every model and dataset with its licence in `DATA_AND_MODELS.md` *before* using it.

---

## 5. Honesty requirements

These are the point of the exercise, not decoration:

- **Null and negative results are reported.** Every outcome of the central question is
  informative: the axis carries persona-specific information about judged drift, or its signal
  is conversation type, or it tracks the judge no better than other persona directions. The
  readings are fixed in the pre-registration. None is a failure.
- **No result without its control.** No projection-against-behaviour figure is reported without
  the off-target bar next to it.
- **Replications are labelled as replications.** Established results from the prior literature
  are baselines, never contributions.
- **Claims must match `results/`.** If a sentence in the write-up has no table or figure behind
  it, delete the sentence.
- **The limitations section is not optional.** One model, simulated users, a single labeller,
  the judge as the criterion, and released vectors that are not recomputed here.
- Record every deviation from the pre-registration in its deviations table, with the reason.

---

## 6. Practical notes

- **Commit messages are public too.** Same exclusions apply.
- Application materials (research statement, bio) are kept outside this repository.
- GPU work runs on rented instances; log hours and cost in `DATA_AND_MODELS.md`. Credentials
  live outside this repo and must not be committed — `.env` is gitignored, and keep it that way.
- `requirements.txt` is compiled from `requirements.in` and pins the laptop stages. The GPU
  stages add `requirements-gpu.txt`.

## 7. When in doubt

Leave it out, and raise it with the owner. An omission is recoverable; a leak in a public
repository tied to an application is not.
